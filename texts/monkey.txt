In a room with no walls, no ceiling, and no end, there sat an infinite number of monkeys at an infinite number of typewriters. Most of them typed lazily, banging out things like "hhhhjjkkk bnnnn" before stopping for a banana.

But not Monkey #4,817,293,006. His friends called him Pip.

Pip had heard the rumor that ran through the endless rows: "Given enough time, one of us will type the complete works of Shakespeare."

"Enough time?" Pip muttered. "I don't want enough time. I want to be first."

So Pip began to train.

Every morning he did finger push-ups. He stretched his toes, because a monkey has four hands, and Pip meant to use all of them. He practiced on a banana peel until he could hit keys with his tail. The monkey on his left, a sleepy old fellow named Bartholomew, watched him and yawned.

"Why rush, Pip? Infinity is very long. Someone will do it eventually."

"Exactly," said Pip, fingers flying. "Someone. And that someone will be me."

Pip typed at 400 words per minute. Then 800. Then so fast that his typewriter started to smoke and the keys glowed orange. Paper piled up around him like snowdrifts.

He read his pages hopefully:

"Tobb or nkt ro bee."

"So close!" he cried.

He typed faster. Weeks passed, or maybe centuries; it's hard to tell in infinity. Then one day, trembling, he pulled out a page and read:

"To be, or not to be, that is the question."

Pip screamed. He jumped on his chair. He did three backflips. "I DID IT! I'M THE FIRST! I'M THE—"

Then he read the next line:

"Whether 'tis nobler in the mind to suffer the slings and bananas of outrageous fortune."

Bananas. He'd written bananas.

Pip sat down very slowly.

From somewhere far down the infinite row, a small voice called out: "Hey, has anyone got more paper? I just finished Hamlet. Starting Macbeth now."

Pip looked at Bartholomew. Bartholomew looked at Pip.

"Infinity," said Bartholomew gently, "is a very crowded place."

Pip was quiet for a long moment. Then he cracked his knuckles, all twenty of them, and rolled a fresh sheet into his smoking typewriter.

"Fine," he said. "Then I'll be the first to write something better than Shakespeare."

And he began to type:

"Once upon a time, there was a monkey who wanted to type faster than anybody..."
